Ask a support lead what fills their queue and they will say where-is-my-order. They're right about the volume. They're usually wrong about the cost.

WISMO tickets are numerous and cheap. The answer is a lookup, an agent can close one in under a minute, and a decent tracking page removes most of them before they are ever sent. We have written about that [separately](/blog-wismo).

The tickets that hurt are the other ones.

## Why a claim costs more than ten WISMO tickets

A claim or a disputed return can't be answered by looking something up. Somebody has to decide.

Is this within policy. Has this customer done this before. Is the photograph convincing. Does the carrier scan support the story. Do we approve it, decline it, or ask for more and risk annoying someone who is telling the truth. That is judgement, and judgement is expensive because it needs your most experienced person, not your newest one.

Then it doesn't close. It becomes a thread. The customer replies, the carrier is chased, a replacement is arranged, finance wants to know why. One claim can generate a week of small interruptions, each one pulling somebody out of what they were doing.

## The cost that never appears in a report

Ticket counts flatter this category badly. A queue of two hundred WISMO messages and fifteen claims looks like a WISMO problem. In hours consumed it frequently isn't.

And the fifteen are the ones with money attached, the ones that turn into chargebacks when they go badly, and the ones your senior people are handling instead of the work you hired them for.

## Self-service that collects a form is not self-service

Plenty of tools offer a claims portal. Most of them are just a form that lands in the same inbox with better formatting. The ticket still arrives, the decision still has to be made, and the only thing that's improved is the tidiness of the queue.

The tickets you actually want are the ones that never open, because the resolution happened at the moment the customer reported the problem. That needs two things a form can't do on its own: rules good enough to decide the clear cases without a person, and somebody other than your team to work the cases that genuinely need judgement.

## What is left afterwards

Nobody removes this category entirely, and you should be suspicious of anyone who says they can. Some claims are ambiguous. Some customers are lying. Some situations need a person who understands your brand to make a call.

What you can change is the mix. If the clear cases resolve themselves and the ambiguous ones are worked by people whose actual job that is, what reaches your team is the small remainder that genuinely needed you. That's a queue a support lead can manage. The one they've got now usually isn't.
